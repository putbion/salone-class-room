PRIMARY • Examination Preparation

NPSE Exam Success Guide

Practical guidance for pupils preparing to sit the National Primary School Examination (NPSE).

1 BEFORE THE EXAM

  • Start preparing early: Begin revision weeks in advance to build understanding and confidence.
  • Read and revise properly: Review class notes, textbook summaries, and formulas steadily.
  • Practice past questions: Answer sample and past questions regularly under timed conditions.
  • Understand rather than simply memorize: Focus on concepts so you can solve varied questions.
  • Prepare materials beforehand: Pack your pencils, pens, ruler, eraser, and exam slip the night before.
  • Sleep adequately: Rest well the night before so your mind is alert and refreshed on exam day.
  • Wake up early: Give yourself ample time to get ready without rushing or stress.
  • Eat appropriately: Eat a light, nourishing meal before going to maintain steady energy.
  • Arrive at the centre early: Arrive at least 45 minutes early to locate your seat calmly.
  • Avoid last-minute panic: Trust your preparation and stay composed before the paper begins.

2 DURING THE EXAMINATION

  • Listen carefully to instructions: Pay close attention to what the invigilators and supervisors say.
  • Read examination instructions: Check the number of questions to answer and where to record answers.
  • Read every question carefully: Understand exactly what each question asks before choosing or writing an answer.
  • Manage your time: Pace yourself so you have sufficient time for all questions.
  • Start with questions you understand: Answering familiar questions first secures marks and boosts confidence.
  • Write clearly: Use legible handwriting so examiners can read every word and number.
  • Number answers correctly: Ensure each response matches the correct question number.
  • Review your work: If time permits, check calculations, spelling, and answered questions carefully.
  • Remain calm and focused: Keep your attention fixed entirely on your own paper.

3 WHAT NOT TO DO IN THE EXAMINATION HALL

  • Do not spy or copy: Peeking at another candidate's paper leads to penalties and wrong answers.
  • Do not allow others to copy: Allowing another candidate to copy makes you equally guilty of malpractice.
  • Do not communicate answers: Whispering, passing notes, or making gestures is strictly forbidden.
  • Do not carry unauthorized materials: Never bring cheat sheets, unauthorized paper, or electronic devices into the hall.
  • Do not write identical copied answers: Examiners easily detect collusion and will cancel results.
  • Do not engage in examination malpractice: Malpractice ruins reputations and cancels exam entries.
  • Do not depend on cheating: Honest, thorough preparation is the only true key to success.

4 CALM & ALERTNESS TIPS

Simple, natural ways to feel calm and alert before the exam begins:

  • Stretch gently: Loosen your neck, arms, and back to release tension.
  • Take calm breaths: Breathe in slowly and exhale steadily to relax your pulse.
  • Rub your palms together: Rubbing your hands together briefly can help you feel grounded and alert.
  • Sit comfortably: Sit upright with your back supported and feet flat on the floor.
  • Relax your shoulders: Drop your shoulders away from your ears to release muscle tightness.
  • Drink water where permitted: Small sips keep you hydrated and comfortable.
  • Focus on your own paper: Keep your thoughts centred on your exam and not on others.
These practical habits help you feel composed, alert, and ready to do your best.

JSS • Examination Preparation

BECE Exam Success Guide

Practical advice and examination strategies for Junior Secondary School candidates sitting the BECE.

1 BEFORE THE EXAM

  • Start preparing early: Create a daily revision timetable covering all core and elective subjects.
  • Read and revise properly: Go beyond surface reading; summarise topics in your own words.
  • Practice past questions: Solve past BECE questions to understand WAEC question patterns.
  • Understand rather than simply memorize: Grasp principles and formulas so you can apply them flexibly.
  • Prepare materials beforehand: Ensure you have your mathematical set, pens, pencils, eraser, and candidate ID ready.
  • Sleep adequately: Get at least 7–8 hours of restful sleep before examination day.
  • Wake up early: Have plenty of time for breakfast and personal preparation.
  • Eat appropriately: Eat balanced food that sustains your energy without causing discomfort.
  • Arrive at the centre early: Be at the examination centre at least 45 minutes prior to inspection.
  • Avoid last-minute panic: Remain calm and avoid frantically opening notebooks right before entering.

2 DURING THE EXAMINATION

  • Listen carefully to instructions: Listen attentively to invigilators before opening question papers.
  • Read examination instructions: Verify time limits, mandatory questions, and section options.
  • Read every question carefully: Pay attention to keywords like "Explain", "List", "Calculate", or "Contrast".
  • Manage your time: Divide your time according to the marks allocated to each question.
  • Start with questions you understand: Build early confidence and secure strong marks first.
  • Write clearly: Neat presentations make your working and points easy to award marks for.
  • Number answers correctly: Check that your answer sheet numbers match the exact question numbers.
  • Review your work: Reserve 10 minutes at the end to check through all your answers and corrections.
  • Remain calm and focused: Maintain composure and concentrate on your own test paper.

3 WHAT NOT TO DO IN THE EXAMINATION HALL

  • Do not spy or copy: Looking around or copying from peers leads to immediate disqualification.
  • Do not allow others to copy: Assisting another candidate makes both parties liable for penalty.
  • Do not communicate answers: No whispering, signals, or sharing of stationery during the test.
  • Do not carry unauthorized materials: Leave all unauthorized notes, books, and phones outside.
  • Do not write identical copied answers: WAEC marking software detects identical phrasing and cancels scripts.
  • Do not engage in examination malpractice: Malpractice brings disciplinary actions and disqualification.
  • Do not depend on cheating: Trust your preparation and write your exam with dignity and honesty.

4 CALM & ALERTNESS TIPS

Simple non-medical preparation habits to keep you calm and alert:

  • Stretch gently: Release shoulder and neck tension with gentle movements.
  • Take calm breaths: Take three deep breaths in and out before picking up your pen.
  • Rub your palms together: Rubbing your hands together briefly can help you feel alert and present.
  • Sit comfortably: Maintain good posture with your back upright and shoulders down.
  • Relax your shoulders: Consciously un-clench your muscles and ease your grip on your pen.
  • Drink water where permitted: A sip of water keeps your concentration clear.
  • Focus on your own paper: Keep your attention fixed strictly on your own desk.
These practical habits help you stay poised, focused, and ready to succeed.

SSS • Examination Preparation

WASSCE Exam Success Guide

Practical advice and master strategies for candidates preparing for the West African Senior School Certificate Examination (WASSCE).

1 BEFORE THE EXAM

  • Start preparing early: Create a thorough study schedule that covers the entire WAEC syllabus systematically.
  • Read and revise properly: Use textbooks, syllabus guides, and well-organized study notes.
  • Practice past questions: Work through past WASSCE papers, testing both speed and depth of working.
  • Understand rather than simply memorize: Examiners test real application, analysis, and problem-solving.
  • Prepare materials beforehand: Pack approved pens, pencils, mathematical instruments, non-programmable calculators, and your photo card.
  • Sleep adequately: Get sound sleep every night during exam season to maintain mental stamina.
  • Wake up early: Allow plenty of calm preparation time without stressful early-morning rush.
  • Eat appropriately: Eat healthy, balanced food to sustain hours of intense concentration.
  • Arrive at the centre early: Be seated at the centre at least 45 minutes before the start time.
  • Avoid last-minute panic: Avoid cramming outside the hall. Take a quiet moment to focus your mind.

2 DURING THE EXAMINATION

  • Listen carefully to instructions: Follow invigilator instructions and read all rubric notes on the cover page.
  • Read examination instructions: Check the required number of questions to answer in each section.
  • Read every question carefully: Note action verbs (e.g., "Critique", "Distinguish", "Prove", "Illustrate").
  • Manage your time: Track the clock and allocate time proportionally to the mark value.
  • Start with questions you understand: Answering your strongest questions first secures solid marks.
  • Write clearly: Structure essays with clear paragraphs, headings, and legible handwriting.
  • Number answers correctly: Ensure sub-parts (e.g. 2a, 2b(i)) are clearly and accurately numbered.
  • Review your work: Leave time to review calculations, graphs, units, and conclusion points.
  • Remain calm and focused: Keep your attention fixed entirely on your desk and your own progress.

3 WHAT NOT TO DO IN THE EXAMINATION HALL

  • Do not spy or copy: Looking around or copying leads to immediate disqualification and cancelled results.
  • Do not allow others to copy: Allowing another candidate to copy makes both candidates equally guilty.
  • Do not communicate answers: Whispering, passing paper, or making signals is strictly prohibited.
  • Do not carry unauthorized materials: Never bring cheat notes, phones, smartwatches, or papers inside.
  • Do not write identical copied answers: WAEC examiners cross-check scripts for collusion across centres.
  • Do not engage in examination malpractice: Malpractice permanently blacklists candidates and cancels results.
  • Do not depend on cheating: Honest, disciplined preparation is the only foundation for true success.

4 CALM & ALERTNESS TIPS

Simple, natural ways to stay composed, sharp, and focused:

  • Stretch gently: Loosen your neck, wrists, and shoulders to relieve physical tension.
  • Take calm breaths: Inhale deeply through the nose and exhale slowly to steady your pulse.
  • Rub your palms together: Rubbing your hands together briefly can help you feel grounded and alert.
  • Sit comfortably: Keep an upright posture with your feet planted flat for optimal oxygen flow.
  • Relax your shoulders: Consciously drop tension from your shoulders and neck.
  • Drink water where permitted: Hydration maintains cognitive clarity and stamina.
  • Focus on your own paper: Keep your thoughts and energy completely on your own desk.
These practical habits help you stay poised, clear-headed, and prepared to excel.

4. TEACHERS GUIDE

Teachers Guide

Practical, respectful guidance on professional conduct, learner differences, positive discipline, conflict management, hygiene, and effective teaching.

▼

👔 Professional Appearance and Dress

▼

Teachers are respected professional role models in the school and wider community. Your presentation sets an example of self-respect and order.

  • Dress neatly and cleanly: Ensure clothing is washed, pressed, and in good condition. Professionalism is about cleanliness, dignity, and modesty—not expensive clothes.
  • Personal hygiene: Maintain high standards of personal cleanliness and grooming every day.
  • Appropriate school clothing: Wear clothing that is suitable for the school environment and respectful of pupils and colleagues.
  • Follow school dress policies: Comply with any official dress code or guidelines established by your school or educational authority.
  • Practical footwear: Choose comfortable and practical shoes suitable for walking, standing, and classroom duties throughout the school day.
  • Avoid distracting attire: Avoid clothing with offensive slogans, overly casual sportswear, or transparent materials that distract from teaching.

🧠 Understanding Learner Differences

▼

Every classroom contains pupils with diverse abilities, backgrounds, and learning styles. Recognizing these differences is the foundation of effective teaching.

  • Different learning speeds: Learners do not absorb information at the identical pace. Some require multiple explanations, while others grasp concepts quickly.
  • Different prior knowledge: Pupils arrive with unequal foundations depending on previous schooling, home support, and attendance.
  • Varied confidence levels: Quiet or hesitant pupils often understand more than they voice; they need gentle encouragement to participate.
  • Eliminate insulting labels: Never refer to a pupil as "stupid", "dull", "incapable", "hopeless", or "lazy". Such derogatory terms damage a child's dignity and permanently extinguish enthusiasm for learning.
  • Focus on progress: Measure each pupil against their own past performance and encourage consistent personal improvement.

⏳ Supporting Learners Who Need More Time (Slow Learners)

▼

Pupils who take longer to learn need structured, compassionate pedagogical support rather than criticism.

  • Practise patience: Maintain a calm, encouraging demeanour when explaining difficult concepts multiple times.
  • Simplify language: Rephrase complex academic terminology using clear, everyday words that pupils easily understand.
  • Break tasks into small steps: Divide difficult exercises into small, achievable milestones to build gradual mastery.
  • Use familiar local examples: Connect abstract concepts to familiar things pupils see in everyday Sierra Leone life.
  • Demonstrate visually: Use drawings on the chalkboard, gestures, or real objects to illustrate what you are explaining.
  • Frequent comprehension checks: Ask quick, friendly questions to verify understanding before moving to the next topic.
  • Never humiliate: Never mock a wrong answer or allow other pupils to laugh. Treat mistakes as natural stepping stones to understanding.

🚀 Engaging Fast and Advanced Learners

▼

Advanced pupils complete regular classwork quickly and can become bored or disruptive if not appropriately stimulated.

  • Extension questions: Prepare higher-order questions that require deeper analysis, evaluation, or creative problem solving.
  • Additional challenges: Provide extension exercises or supplementary reading while peers complete core exercises.
  • Explain reasoning: Ask advanced pupils to explain *how* and *why* they arrived at their answer, deepening their cognitive grasp.
  • Constructive peer mentoring: Where appropriate, allow advanced pupils to pair with and support peers in a respectful, friendly manner.
  • Prevent domination: Ensure advanced pupils do not dominate every classroom discussion or make other pupils feel inferior.

⚖️ Supporting the Middle Group

▼

Teachers often inadvertently direct attention only to the highest achievers and the struggling pupils, leaving the majority in the middle unnoticed.

  • Active engagement: Regularly call on pupils in the middle group by name to answer questions and contribute ideas.
  • Constructive feedback: Review their work thoroughly and provide specific written comments on how they can move up to higher mastery.
  • Encourage ambition: Inspire middle pupils to believe they can attain excellence with consistent practice and curiosity.
  • Celebrate steady progress: Recognize consistent effort, neat classwork, and incremental improvements in test scores.

🤝 Inclusive Classroom Practice

▼

An inclusive classroom welcomes every pupil regardless of gender, socio-economic background, physical ability, or personal background.

  • Gender equality: Call equally upon boys and girls for answers, leadership roles, chalkboard writing, and science experiments.
  • Accommodating physical needs: Seat pupils with visual or hearing impairments near the front and close to the chalkboard. Ensure wheelchair or crutch access is unobstructed.
  • Fair treatment for all: Treat pupils from all socio-economic, linguistic, and cultural backgrounds with equal dignity and respect.
  • Professional referral boundaries: Teachers must not attempt medical or psychological diagnoses. If a pupil shows signs of developmental delays, severe vision/hearing impairment, or trauma, report observations objectively to the school principal or guidance counsellor following official procedures.

📋 Classroom Management and Positive Discipline

▼

Effective classroom management creates a calm, structured atmosphere where teaching and learning can flourish safely.

  • Establish clear expectations: Agree on concise, positive classroom rules at the start of the term and review them regularly.
  • Start lessons promptly: Enter the classroom on time and begin immediately with an engaging starter activity to set a purposeful tone.
  • Keep pupils engaged: Active lessons with varied activities naturally minimize restlessness, boredom, and classroom disruption.
  • Remain calm under pressure: Address misbehaviour firmly and quietly without shouting, losing temper, or creating dramatic scenes.
  • Correct without humiliating: Never demean a child in front of the whole class. Speak to the pupil privately where possible.
  • Follow school disciplinary policies: Administer legitimate, fair, and educational disciplinary measures approved by your school. Never use harsh physical or humiliating punishments.

🕊️ Conflict Management & Resolution (Fighting & Disagreements)

▼

When disagreements, shouting, or physical confrontations occur between pupils, follow this structured, peaceful resolution approach:

  1. Stay calm: Take a deep breath. Your composure signals that the situation is under control.
  2. Prioritize immediate safety: Step in quickly, order pupils to stop, and physically separate the parties to prevent injury.
  3. Allow tempers to cool: Give the pupils a few moments of silence to calm down before questioning them.
  4. Listen impartially: Hear each pupil's account one at a time without allowing interruptions or passing premature judgements.
  5. Establish facts: Ask neutral questions: "What happened first?", "What did you feel?", "What could have been done differently?".
  6. Remind pupils of mutual respect: Explain that disagreements are normal, but violence, insults, and revenge are unacceptable.
  7. Guide them to a peaceful resolution: Help the pupils apologize where appropriate, shake hands or acknowledge mutual responsibility.
  8. Follow school referral procedures: Document serious incidents involving physical harm or weapons and refer them immediately to the school head teacher or disciplinary committee.

🧹 Classroom Hygiene, Health & Safety (First Aid & Large Classes)

▼

A clean, well-ordered classroom protects pupils' health and promotes mental focus and productivity.

  • Clean and swept environment: Ensure the classroom floor is swept daily, waste bins are emptied, and chalk dust is minimized.
  • Ventilation and lighting: Keep windows and doors open to allow fresh air and natural sunlight to circulate throughout the day.
  • Clear walkways: Keep aisles free of schoolbags, loose benches, and tripping hazards to ensure safe movement and quick exit during an emergency.
  • First aid awareness: Know the location of the school first aid box. For minor cuts or scrapes, clean the wound and apply a sterile dressing. For severe fever, fainting, vomiting, or injury, immediately alert the school health coordinator or head teacher.
  • Managing large classes: In classes with 50+ pupils, organize the room into distinct rows or cooperative groups with student row monitors. Use clear hand signals or rhythmic clapping rather than straining your voice to call the class to attention.

⭐ Teacher Professional Judgement & Mutual Respect

▼

"Salone Class Room supports the teacher; it does not replace the teacher."

  • Professional review & adaptation: Generated lesson notes, exam questions, and teaching guides are helpful drafts. Always review, adapt, and tailor them to the specific abilities, pace, and context of your pupils.
  • Respect school authority and curriculum: Comply with your school's scheme of work, head teacher's instructions, and the official Ministry of Basic and Senior Secondary Education (MBSSE) syllabus.
  • Mutual respect: Model dignity, fairness, and encouragement in every interaction. When pupils feel genuinely valued by their teacher, their academic confidence and good conduct naturally grow.

About Salone Class Room

Why Salone Class Room was created, how it can support learning and teaching, and the important limitations every user should understand.

Why We Created Salone Class Room

Technology has changed how people find information, communicate, and learn. Today, many pupils, students, and teachers spend more time using mobile phones, laptops, and other digital devices than visiting traditional libraries. Salone Class Room was created to bring useful educational support closer to people through the devices they already use.

By making learning resources easier to access online, we hope to encourage pupils and students to use part of their time on digital devices for meaningful learning, revision, and academic development, rather than being distracted by online content that does not contribute positively to their education.

Supporting Accessible Education in Sierra Leone

Salone Class Room is designed to complement the Government of Sierra Leone's efforts to make education more accessible and affordable. Our goal is to provide additional learning and teaching support to Sierra Leoneans regardless of where they live. As long as reliable internet access is available, a learner or teacher can use Salone Class Room to obtain educational guidance from a phone, tablet, laptop or desktop computer.

This is particularly important for learners and teachers in communities where access to libraries, textbooks and other educational resources may be limited.

Supporting Teachers

Salone Class Room also provides practical support for teachers. It can help teachers prepare lesson notes and prepare theory examination questions for classroom assessment. It may also be useful to individuals supporting learning who can read and write effectively but have had limited access to formal teacher training, helping them prepare more carefully and make a more positive contribution in the classroom.

The platform, however, does not provide a teaching qualification and should not be regarded as a substitute for professional teacher training, approved curricula, official textbooks, teacher guides or other materials prescribed by the relevant education authorities.

Why We Encourage Writing Instead of Direct Download

Salone Class Room intentionally does not make direct downloading the main way of collecting learning materials. Our aim is to encourage active learning rather than simply allowing pupils, students and teachers to download information without carefully engaging with it.

When learners read useful information and write or copy the important points into their notebooks, they spend more time thinking about what they are learning. Writing can also support concentration, note-taking habits, revision and better understanding. For teachers, carefully transferring and adapting useful material into their lesson notes can encourage proper preparation before teaching a lesson.

This approach is not intended to make learning unnecessarily difficult. It is designed to promote participation, responsibility and good study habits. Users are encouraged to read, understand, select relevant information, and write down what is useful rather than depending only on ready-made downloaded materials.

Important Educational Notice

Salone Class Room is a supplementary educational support platform. It is not a replacement for formal schooling, qualified teachers, lecturers, supervisors, approved textbooks, official curricula, syllabuses or other recognised learning and teaching materials. Users should use the platform together with, and not instead of, the formal educational resources and professional guidance available to them.

Because the platform relies on technology, artificial intelligence and internet connectivity, information may occasionally contain errors, incomplete explanations or technical limitations. Access can also be affected by internet availability, device problems, third-party services or other circumstances beyond the direct control of Salone Class Room. Users are therefore encouraged to verify important academic information against official materials and seek clarification from their teachers or other qualified education professionals where necessary.

Guidance for University Students

The university and research tools on Salone Class Room are intended to provide guidance, help students understand academic processes, and support the organisation of their own work. They should not be used to replace independent academic work or the official requirements of a university.

University students, particularly those working on proposals, research projects, dissertations, or theses, should continue to work closely with their lecturers, research supervisors, and departments. Where guidance from Salone Class Room differs from the requirements of a student's institution or supervisor, the institution's official requirements and the supervisor's guidance should take priority.

Our Purpose

Salone Class Room aims to use technology responsibly to make learning support easier to access, encourage productive use of digital devices and contribute to a more informed and educated Sierra Leone.
